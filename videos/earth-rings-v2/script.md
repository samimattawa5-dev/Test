## Fact list (2+ sources each)
1. Hypothesis: Earth may have had a ring ~466 million years ago (Ordovician); Andy Tomkins et al., Monash University, *Earth and Planetary Science Letters*, Sept 2024 — [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/space/earth-once-wore-a-saturn-like-ring-study-of-ancient-craters-suggests), [EarthSky](https://earthsky.org/earth/did-earth-have-rings-asteroid-ordovician-period), [Space.com](https://space.com/ancient-earth-ring-system-asteroid-breakup)
2. 21 Ordovician impact craters, all within 30° of the equator — [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/space/earth-once-wore-a-saturn-like-ring-study-of-ancient-craters-suggests), [Space.com](https://space.com/ancient-earth-ring-system-asteroid-breakup)
3. Plate-tectonic reconstruction of where the continents were — [The Weather Network](https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/science/space/earth-may-have-once-had-glittering-rings-millions-of-years-ago), [EarthSky](https://earthsky.org/earth/did-earth-have-rings-asteroid-ordovician-period)
4. Only ~30% of the stable continental crust was near the equator — [EarthSky](https://earthsky.org/earth/did-earth-have-rings-asteroid-ordovician-period), [The Weather Network](https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/science/space/earth-may-have-once-had-glittering-rings-millions-of-years-ago)
5. Odds by chance ~1 in 25 million — [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/space/earth-once-wore-a-saturn-like-ring-study-of-ancient-craters-suggests), [Space.com](https://space.com/ancient-earth-ring-system-asteroid-breakup)
6. Rubble-pile asteroid torn apart inside the Roche limit (~3,100 km above the surface) — [The Weather Network](https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/science/space/earth-may-have-once-had-glittering-rings-millions-of-years-ago), [Space.com](https://space.com/ancient-earth-ring-system-asteroid-breakup)
7. Ordovician sedimentary rocks contain unusually large amounts of meteorite debris — [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/space/earth-once-wore-a-saturn-like-ring-study-of-ancient-craters-suggests), [EarthSky](https://earthsky.org/earth/did-earth-have-rings-asteroid-ordovician-period)
8. The Hirnantian cold period was among the coldest of the last 500 million years — [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/space/earth-once-wore-a-saturn-like-ring-study-of-ancient-craters-suggests), [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/space/ordovician-earth-ring-system-13270.html)
9. Ring may have lasted ~40 million years, shading the equator → may have contributed to the Hirnantian cold period — [The Weather Network](https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/science/space/earth-may-have-once-had-glittering-rings-millions-of-years-ago), [EarthSky](https://earthsky.org/earth/did-earth-have-rings-asteroid-ordovician-period)
10. Other scientists say the data are not yet sufficient — [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/space/earth-once-wore-a-saturn-like-ring-study-of-ancient-craters-suggests), [EarthSky](https://earthsky.org/earth/did-earth-have-rings-asteroid-ordovician-period)
