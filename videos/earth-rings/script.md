# Earth Had Rings? — 3D Story Short (draft v1)

Audience: general, English. Length ~61 s, 13 shots, ~160 words.

## Shot table
| # | Time | Voiceover | On screen |
|---|------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | 0–4 | 466 million years ago, Earth may have worn a ring like Saturn. | Space: Earth with a glowing ring, camera swings around it |
| 2 | 4–8 | Scientists didn't find the ring. They found the holes it left behind. | Desert: scientist character with magnifier next to a crater |
| 3 | 8–13 | Twenty-one ancient impact craters, all from the same slice of time. | Desert: rows of craters, camera rises up |
| 4 | 13–18 | And every single one sits close to the equator of that ancient Earth. | Space: Earth with a red equator band, crater dots on it |
| 5 | 18–23 | Random space rocks don't do that. The odds are about one in twenty-five million. | Lab: two scientists, giant dice, one surprised (bounce) |
| 6 | 23–28 | So here's the idea: a big asteroid flew too close to Earth. | Space: rock passes close to Earth, camera follows |
| 7 | 28–33 | Gravity tore it apart, and the pieces spread into a glowing ring. | Space: rock fragments circling, ring forming |
| 8 | 33–38 | For millions of years, chunks of that ring rained down near the equator. | Desert: falling rocks, character running (walk) |
| 9 | 38–43 | Here's the twist: the ring may have cast a giant shadow on the planet. | Space: ring shadow band across Earth |
| 10 | 43–48 | Around the same time, Earth plunged into one of its coldest periods in 500 million years. | Ocean/ice: blue-white world, shivering characters |
| 11 | 48–53 | Did a ring help freeze the planet? Some scientists aren't convinced yet. | Lab: one scientist nods (bounce), one shakes head |

| 13 | 58–61 | Earth might have had one. | Space: slow pull-back from ringed Earth |

## Fact list (each claim, 2+ sources)
1. **Earth may have had a ring ~466 million years ago (Ordovician)** — hypothesis by Andy Tomkins et al., Monash University, *Earth and Planetary Science Letters*, Sept 2024. [Space.com](https://space.com/ancient-earth-ring-system-asteroid-breakup), [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/space/earth-once-wore-a-saturn-like-ring-study-of-ancient-craters-suggests), [EarthSky](https://earthsky.org/earth/did-earth-have-rings-asteroid-ordovician-period)
2. **21 impact craters from that period, all within ~30° of the equator at the time** — [Space.com](https://space.com/ancient-earth-ring-system-asteroid-breakup), [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/space/ordovician-earth-ring-system-13270.html)
3. **Odds of that pattern by chance ~1 in 25 million** — [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/space/earth-once-wore-a-saturn-like-ring-study-of-ancient-craters-suggests), [Space.com](https://space.com/ancient-earth-ring-system-asteroid-breakup)
4. **Large asteroid broke up after crossing Earth's Roche limit, forming the ring** — [Space.com](https://space.com/ancient-earth-ring-system-asteroid-breakup), [EarthSky](https://earthsky.org/earth/did-earth-have-rings-asteroid-ordovician-period)
5. **Ring shadow may have contributed to the Hirnantian cold period, among the coldest of the last 500 million years** — [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/space/earth-once-wore-a-saturn-like-ring-study-of-ancient-craters-suggests), [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/space/ordovician-earth-ring-system-13270.html)
6. **Other scientists say the data aren't sufficient yet** (e.g. Birger Schmitz, Lund University) — [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/space/earth-once-wore-a-saturn-like-ring-study-of-ancient-craters-suggests), [EarthSky](https://earthsky.org/earth/did-earth-have-rings-asteroid-ordovician-period)

Fiction note: cartoon scientists and their reactions are illustrative, not real people. The ring is presented as a hypothesis ("may have", "might"), never as proven fact.
